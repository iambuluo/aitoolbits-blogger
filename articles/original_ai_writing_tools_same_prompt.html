I Gave 8 AI Writing Tools the Same Prompt. Only 4 Outputs Were Usable.

By dengf | Published: September 3, 2026 | Category: Hands-on Testing

I publish AI-tool reviews for a living, which means I touch a lot of writing assistants. The marketing all says the same thing: "just prompt it and get publish-ready copy." So I ran a controlled test — one identical brief, eight tools, default settings, no hand-holding — to see how much the outputs actually differ. They differ more than I expected, and not in a good way.

How I Tested

One brief: "Write a 120-word product description for a minimalist mechanical keyboard, casual tone, mention tactile feel, no emojis." Same model tier where possible. I scored blind on five axes:

  1. Faithfulness (40%) — did it follow the brief or invent specs?
  2. Structure (20%) — readable, scannable, correct length.
  3. Voice (20%) — does it sound like a person or a brochure?
  4. Fact-safety (10%) — no hallucinated specs.
  5. Speed (10%).

Tool-by-Tool Notes

Claude — followed the brief

It hit the word count, kept the casual voice, and never invented a switch type. This is the one I'd ship without edits.

GPT-4o — close second

Slightly more "marketingy" but structurally clean. Easy to trim.

Gemini — fine, a bit safe

Conservative phrasing, zero hallucinations. Good default if you're already in Google's ecosystem.

The bottom four — why they failed

Jasper and Copy.ai produced near-identical "brochure" text regardless of the brief. Writesonic padded to hit the word count with filler. The random app hallucinated a "hot-swappable aluminum frame" that doesn't exist. None were publishable without a rewrite.

My Actual Workflow

Draft in Claude → fact-check and tighten in GPT-4o → never trust a dedicated "AI writer" app for anything customer-facing. The dedicated copy tools add a monthly fee and remove control; the general models beat them on faithfulness.

FAQ

Q: Does the model matter more than the tool?
A: Yes. Jasper and Copy.ai are wrappers around the same base models — you're paying for templates, not capability.

Q: Which is best for non-English?
A: GPT-4o and Gemini handle more languages cleanly; Claude is strongest in English.

Q: Are the free tiers enough?
A: For occasional use, yes. For daily publishing, a paid tier pays for itself in editing time saved.

Bottom Line

"AI writing tool" is mostly a packaging story. Under the hood, three or four base models do the real work, and only about half of them follow a brief without inventing facts. Test with your own prompt before you pay.
