Turn Any Blog Post Into a LinkedIn Carousel With AI (No Design Skills)

By dengf | Published: September 3, 2026 | Category: Step-by-step Tutorial

A carousel (those swipeable PDFs on LinkedIn) consistently beats a plain link post for reach. The problem: designing ten slides by hand is a chore. Here's the exact workflow I use to turn one published blog post into a ready-to-post carousel in about 20 minutes, using AI for the words and a free tool for the layout.

What You Need

  • One blog post you've already published (the source of truth).
  • An AI chat tool (any of the major ones).
  • A free carousel maker (Canva's free tier, or a markdown-to-PDF tool).

Step 1 — Extract the spine

Prompt the AI: "Summarize this post as 7 standalone takeaways, each one sentence, no filler." You want seven because LinkedIn carousels perform best at 7–10 slides. Paste the post text, not the URL — models summarize your pasted text more reliably than they fetch a page.

Step 2 — Write the slide copy

For each takeaway, prompt: "Turn this into a slide: a 6-word headline + 2 bullet points, casual tone." Keep each slide to one idea. If a slide has three bullets, it's doing too much — split it.

Step 3 — Add a hook and a close

Slide 1 is not a takeaway — it's the promise. Prompt: "Write a slide-1 hook that names the reader's problem in 8 words." Slide 10 is the CTA: "Write a closing slide: one question that invites a comment." A carousel with a question at the end gets dramatically more replies than one that just ends.

Step 4 — Lay it out

Paste the 10 slides into your carousel maker. Rules I follow: one idea per slide, 24pt+ text, lots of whitespace, same accent color on every slide. If a slide feels crowded, cut half of it. Consistency beats cleverness here.

Step 5 — Post with context

Don't just upload the PDF. Write a one-line caption that states the payoff, and reply to early comments within the first hour — that's what pushes it to more feeds.

Common Pitfalls

MistakeFix
Too much text per slideOne idea, max 3 short bullets
Generic hookName a specific pain in 8 words
No CTAEnd with a question, not a summary
Inconsistent designSame color + font on all slides

FAQ

Q: Do I need the blog post first?
A: Not strictly, but starting from a real post keeps the carousel accurate instead of generic.

Q: Which maker is best?
A: Canva free tier is enough. Don't pay for carousel-specific SaaS — the layout is trivial once the copy is done.

Q: How long does it take?
A: ~20 minutes once the prompt routine is set. The first one takes longer; the tenth is automatic.

Bottom Line

The AI does the writing; you do the editing and the layout discipline. Ten slides from one post, no design degree, and it reaches more people than the original link ever did.
