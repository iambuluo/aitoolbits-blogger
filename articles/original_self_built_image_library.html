I Replaced My $60/Month Stock Subscription With a Self-Built Image Library — The Honest Build Log

By dengf  |  Published: July 15, 2026  |  Category: Hands-on Testing

I run a blog that publishes a lot of posts, and for years I paid a stock-photo subscription — about $60/month — so every article could have a decent hero image. Then I did what anyone who builds image tools for a living would do: I wired the free stock APIs directly into a WordPress plugin and stopped paying. This is the honest log of what happened, including the part where it didn't fully work.

The Setup

I publish roughly 60 images a month across the blog (about 3 per post, some posts more). At typical paid rates of $0.50–$3.00 per image, that subscription was actually the cheap end — à-la-carte would have been $30–$180/month on its own. So $60 felt "reasonable" right up until I realized most of what I needed wasn't premium at all.

What I Built

The plugin is simple on purpose:

  • A search box in the post editor that queries Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay at once.
  • Results show the real license, so I only one-click-insert permissive images.
  • On insert, the image downloads into my media library (no hot-linking — see my other post on that mistake).
  • Everything is cached, so repeated searches don't burn API quota.

That's it. No AI, no magic. Just the free APIs I was already browsing manually, now one keystroke from the editor.

The Real Numbers

After a month: about 70% of my images came straight from the free APIs. The remaining 30% — specific hero shots, a particular model, an on-brand color I couldn't find — I bought à la carte, averaging around $18. Net cost dropped from $60 to ~$18 a month. That's roughly $42 saved every month, compounding to $500+/year, for an afternoon of building.

Where It Fell Short (The Honest Part)

Free sources are not a perfect substitute. Two real gaps:

  • Curation fatigue. Free libraries are huge but uneven. I spend more time skimming for a non-generic shot than I did with a curated paid catalog. For high-traffic hero images I still care about quality, so I'll hunt longer.
  • Rare subjects. When I need something specific — a niche product, a particular pose, an exclusive scene — free results often don't exist. Those I buy, and I'm fine with that.

So the honest verdict: I didn't replace paid images entirely. I replaced the subscription and kept paid as a targeted top-up. For my volume, that's the sweet spot.

Would I Recommend It?

If you publish a lot and most of your images are generic (blog headers, section art, decorative shots), yes — build or install a free-API plugin and cancel the subscription. If your brand lives on very specific, exclusive imagery, keep a paid plan and just use the plugin to fill the gaps. Either way, you stop paying $60/month for images you could have pulled for free.

FAQ

Is this against the stock sites' terms?
No — Unsplash, Pexels, and Pixabay explicitly allow free commercial use. I only insert images whose license permits it, and I never hot-link.

How long did it take to build?
An afternoon for a basic version. The caching and license-display parts took longer than the API calls themselves.

What about image quality for SEO?
I still compress and add alt text on insert. Free source doesn't mean skipping basic on-page hygiene — that matters more for rankings than where the pixel came from.
