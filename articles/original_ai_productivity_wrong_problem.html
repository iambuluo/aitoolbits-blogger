Most "AI Productivity" Tools Are Solving the Wrong Problem

By dengf | Published: September 3, 2026 | Category: Opinion

Every week a new "AI productivity" tool lands in my inbox promising to save me hours. I've tried dozens. My honest take: most of them optimize a step that was never the bottleneck. They make the easy part faster and leave the hard part exactly where it was.

The Pattern I Keep Seeing

The bottleneck in real work is rarely "typing the words" or "drawing the box." It's deciding what to make, for whom, and why it matters. That's the part no tool touches — and it's the part that actually consumes the hours. So a tool that "writes the draft 10x faster" saves you the 8 minutes you didn't need saving, while the 3 hours of deciding what to say are untouched.

Three Examples

AI meeting note-takers

They transcribe perfectly. But the real cost of a meeting was never the notes — it was the 40 minutes you spent in it, plus the 20 deciding who does what. The transcript arrives in seconds and then sits unread.

AI slide generators

They lay out bullets fast. But the hard part was figuring out the one point the slide must make. A pretty deck of the wrong message is worse, not better.

AI inbox triage

It sorts your email. But the drag was writing the replies, not sorting. Now you have a tidy inbox of things you still have to think about.

Where AI Actually Helps

The tools worth keeping do one of two things: they remove a genuine mechanical tax (transcription, resizing, translation), or they shorten a feedback loop (draft → critique → revise in seconds). Notice both are about the work around the thinking, not the thinking itself.

The Counter-Argument

Some will say speed compounds — shaving 8 minutes off 50 tasks is 6 hours. Fair. But if those 50 tasks were low-value to begin with, you've efficiently done the wrong things. Throughput on the wrong work is still the wrong work.

FAQ

Q: Are any AI productivity tools worth it?
A: The ones that remove a mechanical tax or shorten a real loop. Skip the ones that just "make drafting faster."

Q: How do I tell before buying?
A: Ask "what hour does this save — the thinking hour or the typing hour?" If it's the typing hour, pass.

Q: Doesn't faster drafting free up thinking time?
A: Only if you then think. Most people fill the saved time with more low-value drafts.

Bottom Line

Before you adopt the next "save hours" tool, name the exact hour it saves. If that hour isn't the one that hurts, it's a distraction with a dashboard.
